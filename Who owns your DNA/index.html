SECTION 02

YOUR DNA. YOUR DATA.
WHO CONTROLS IT?

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) contains the genetic instructions that influence biological development and function. When sequenced, this information becomes genomic data — a digital representation of your genetic code.

Genomic data can support research into diseases, inherited conditions, and responses to medicines. Genetic testing companies and research institutions generate, process, and store this data, which has significant scientific and commercial value.

"If our DNA data has value, should we have more control over how it is used?"

SECTION 03

THE OWNERSHIP GAP

Research Question: How can individuals exercise meaningful control over genomic data access while enabling secure, consent-based research and potential compensation?

👁️

Limited Visibility

Individuals often have little insight into how their genetic data is subsequently used after collection.

🔒

Privacy Risks

Genetic information is uniquely sensitive — it can reveal health predispositions and family relationships.

⚠️

Unauthorized Sharing

Data breaches and undisclosed third-party sharing create risks beyond the original consent.

💰

Compensation Uncertainty

When genetic data contributes to commercial research, individuals rarely share in the economic value.

SECTION 04

TRUST, WITHOUT BLIND TRUST

Blockchain technology offers properties that could address some of the data-control challenges in genomics:

→Transparency — Recorded transactions visible to authorized participants
→Traceability — Access and permission logs create an audit trail
→Consent Management — Authorization records linked to specific permissions
→Smart Contracts — Predefined conditions for access and payment
→Accountability — Tamper-evident records for auditable events

⚠️ Important: Blockchain does not automatically guarantee privacy, legal ownership, or fraud prevention. It is one component in a broader governance framework.

SECTION 07

CAN YOUR GENETIC DATA GENERATE VALUE?

MODEL 01

Research Data Access

Eligible participants permit research use under agreed conditions. Research organizations may pay for access to eligible datasets.

MODEL 02

Genomic Data Cooperatives

Individuals pool data and collectively negotiate access terms and possible compensation.

MODEL 03

Consent-Based Research Partnerships

Participants agree to specific projects and may receive compensation if the program provides it.

PARTICIPANT → CONSENT → APPROVED RESEARCH → AGREED COMPENSATION

⚠️ Compensation is not guaranteed. Data value varies, and many research programs do not directly pay participants.

SECTION 09

THE PRICE OF TRUST

🔴Privacy

Genetic data reveals sensitive information about individuals and relatives

🛡️

Encryption, access controls, off-chain storage

🔴Legal Ownership

Digital records do not automatically establish legal data ownership

🛡️

Legal agreements, regulatory compliance

🔴Irreversibility

Blockchain records are difficult to correct or remove

🛡️

Minimal on-chain data, off-chain references

🔴Consent Withdrawal

Cannot guarantee deletion of previously obtained copies

🛡️

Clear terms, contractual obligations, withdrawal procedures

🔴Security

Keys, accounts, and storage systems can be compromised

🛡️

Strong authentication, key management, audits

🔴Fair Compensation

Individual data contribution value is hard to determine

🛡️

Transparent models, collective bargaining

🔴Regulation

Privacy and genetic data laws vary across jurisdictions

🛡️

Legal oversight, ethics review, compliance frameworks

SECTION 11

YOUR DNA MAY HAVE VALUE.
YOUR RIGHTS MATTER.

✦ Blockchain could help manage access to genomic data through transparent permission records, auditable transactions, and smart contracts.

✦ However, blockchain alone cannot guarantee privacy, legal ownership, control over previously copied data, or financial returns.

✦ A responsible implementation must combine technology with informed consent, legal safeguards, secure storage, and research ethics.

"Your DNA may have research value. Meaningful control requires more than blockchain."
